Where we go deep

Six areas, not a catalogue.

These are where the demand sits right now and where we hold our depth. We would rather be excellent at six things than adequate at twenty, so we turn down work outside them.

01

AI and GenAI enablement

The platform underneath the models: retrieval and vector infrastructure, evaluation, guardrails, and the access controls that let a regulated business put GenAI in front of real users.

02

Cloud modernisation

Moving workloads off legacy estates with a sequencing plan, a rollback story, and an honest view of what should stay where it is.

03

Platform engineering

Internal developer platforms, golden paths, Kubernetes and infrastructure-as-code — the plumbing that makes self-service safe and shortens the path from commit to production.

04

Data engineering and governance

Pipelines, lakehouse and streaming architecture, lineage and quality — the groundwork that decides whether analytics and AI are trustworthy.

05

Cloud security and compliance

Identity, zero-trust network design, policy as code, and evidence that satisfies an auditor without stopping engineers from shipping.

06

Reliability and FinOps

Service levels that mean something, incident and on-call practice, and cost visibility by team and product so spend stays defensible as you scale.

How we work

Four steps, in this order.

The sequence matters. Skipping the assessment is how a programme ends up delivering something nobody can operate.

01

Conversation

Half an hour on what you are trying to change. No deck. If we are not the right firm we will say so and point you somewhere better.

02

Assessment

One to two weeks inside your environment. Findings, a prioritised plan and a cost you can hold us to.

03

Delivery

Consulting, an embedded team, or placed engineers — whichever the plan calls for. Fortnightly demos against the plan, not status reports.

04

Handover

Runbooks, documented decisions and a team trained to operate it. We aim to make the next engagement optional.

Straight answers

The questions we get asked first.

Usually before anyone will say what they actually need. Here they are, answered plainly.

You were founded in 2025. Why take the risk on a young firm?

The firm is new. The people are not — our consultants come out of banking, sovereign wealth and large-scale enterprise infrastructure. We also structure engagements so the first commitment is small: a four to eight week assessment with a fixed fee and a defined output. If the work isn't good, you stop there having lost weeks rather than quarters.

Who actually does the work?

The people who scoped it. We do not sell a senior consultant and staff the delivery with juniors learning on your programme. If the team has to change mid-engagement we tell you before it happens and say why.

How do you price?

Advisory is fixed scope and fixed fee, so a review cannot quietly become a retainer. Delivery is outcome based against the plan the assessment produced. Staffing is a day rate for contract or a placement fee for permanent. No lock-in, no minimum term.

Can you work inside our security and change process?

Yes, and we prefer to. Work happens in your repositories, your pipelines and your ticket queue, under your access model and approvals. It is slower to start and much faster to finish, because there is no shadow estate to reconcile at the end.

What if we only need one of the three?

That's normal. Plenty of clients take an assessment and deliver it themselves, or only ever engage us for staffing. The three are available together because the seams between them cause problems — not because they're sold as a bundle.

How quickly can you start?

A first conversation within a few days. Staffing shortlists land within five working days of a brief. Assessment and delivery start dates depend on the team we need to assemble, and we will give you a real date rather than an optimistic one.
